Crumble: How a Bakery Item became a Luxury Accessory in Pakistan

How a hostel-room cookie business used social media, scarcity and packaging to become a status symbol among Gen Z.

FW
Fawad Waheed Published November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

People sell products all the time; what matters more is how they sell them.

Often, brands sell to the eyes first, not to customers.

Crumble didn’t just become famous and get rapt attention overnight. The sheer occurrence of its fame lies in strategic measures that corroborate to its standard today.

The project that was started by Agha Brothers in 2022 at Ghulam Ishaq Khan Institute’s (GIKI) hostels led to the distinction they hold now. The NYC-style gooey and fudgy cookies were first developed and sold to the University students in order to pay off the tuition fees. Later on, the support they received led to an essential outlet being opened in Pakistan. With several hardships faced by Agha Brothers, the brand quickly rose in about 2 years. In 2024, the brand started getting recognition, and as of 2026, several outlets have been opened across different cities in Pakistan.[1]

The question is simple: Did it perform a specific ritual to get here, or did the brand build itself using certain methodology and strategies?

Of course, the first situation isn’t true at all. The brand built itself by using the biggest tool we see today: marketing through social media. Crumble got its major recognition by selling it to the eyes first rather than selling it to the customers. Who were they selling it to? They were selling it to Gen Z.[2] Through the social media posts where they make satirical and comical content, the product sells immediately. Not only is the originality and creativity of the ideas evident, but also the brand’s social media team follows the trends in its own humorous way. The nonchalance of the brand influences people immediately.[4] Therefore, the blue and white polka dots remind them immediately of the only brand that has the most amusing advertising techniques – Crumble.

It’s unjust to say that Crumble only gets its sales through social media advertising. Rather, the Brand built itself through its own standards and protocols. Bringing in a new taste and genre of desserts was also one of the key factors that got Crumble the reputation it holds today. Through weekly menu changes and creating special cookies that sell out immediately require an amount of strategic strength and creativity.

The facts don’t end there yet. The brand has now become a luxury because we know for a fact that whenever crumble drops a new trend, a new flavor, or even a new product, every influencer is trying it out merely because of the fact that no one wants to be left behind; otherwise, known as Fear of Missing Out. This plays a crucial role in bringing customers to the brand that is based on the scarcity concept taught in textbooks.

Crumble isn’t just a bakery Item; it has found its place among the high-end luxuries that have become a necessity. Someone who hasn’t eaten crumble is treated almost exactly like someone who hasn’t watched ‘Friends’ or ‘Stranger Things’. Therefore, we can say that Crumble has become a necessity for inclusion into the community that believes in following trends.

Before I end the dissection of Crumble’s business, we must enlighten ourselves with one more key aspect of its Marketing and Advertising. Whenever customers buy a product from somewhere, we oftentimes ignore the meaning behind its packaging. This is where Crumble levelled up its game.[3] The packaging became a Luxury bag that customers showed off in their social media posts. What crumble had to do was repost them on its social media account. Therefore, there became an endless loop of a brand pleasing its customers and customers going back to the same outlet to buy more.

What started as a side-gig in a hostel dorm-room quickly kicked off as one of the leading brands selling desserts in Pakistan. Through its strategic marketing and advertising across its social media pages, Crumble became a part of every influencer’s collaboration. The satirical trends immediately brought in the Gen-Zs who took up its image further. That being said, we can say that brands do sell their products; However, what matters more is how they sell them.
