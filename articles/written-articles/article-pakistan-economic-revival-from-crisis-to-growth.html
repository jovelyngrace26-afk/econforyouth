Pakistan Economic Revival: From Crisis to Growth

Decades of boom-and-bust cycles, and three reforms in agriculture, taxation and foreign investment that could put Pakistan’s economy back on track.

AM
Asadullah bin Munir Published November 17, 2026 · 3 min read

We read everyday that Pakistan’s economy is falling. From the time of independence to uptill now, almost 80 years there are still no signs of improvement. The World Bank stated, “Pakistan has a history of slow growth and too frequent boom-bust cycles. Episodes of high economic growth were never sustained and were reversed within a few years.”[1] The reasons are quite simple. Political instability, heavy reliance on debts and financial help from other countries, not really looking at what Pakistan and Pakistani people have to offer for their economy.

So today, we will look into potential changes that certainly will help Pakistan’s economy to get back on track. As the great Iqbal said:

“The destiny of nations lies in the hands of individuals; every individual is a star in the destiny of the nation.”[2]

Possible Solutions

The possible solutions that could be implemented in order to ameliorate the situation of Pakistan’s economic crisis are given below.

Improvement in the Agricultural Sector

Pakistan has always been an agricultural country. The majority of Pakistan’s economy relies on its agricultural department. As the Ministry of Finance, Government of Pakistan, Pakistan Economic Survey 2024–25, Chapter 2: Agriculture states, “Agriculture remains a backbone of Pakistan’s economy, contributing 23.54% to GDP and providing employment to over 37% of the country’s labour force.”[3] The main problem of this department is that it is not that much yielding for a farmer. It cost him his time, money and efforts but in return he gets almost nothing compared to his efforts. The Pakistani government should implement such policies in which a farmer doesn’t feel that he is underpaid. The government should introduce such schemes that lessen the burden over the farmers.

Improving Tax Policies

Another thing that is primarily leading to the economic crisis is Pakistan’s short term policies regarding their taxation. The government uses tax as a temporary tool to get some benefit, not thinking that if they imply taxation in a better way for a long term it will ultimately result in Pakistan’s economic growth. According to IMF research, Pakistan’s tax potential remains underutilised, making comprehensive taxation reforms essential for mobilising domestic resources and financing economic development.[4] Another thing is that FBR (Federal Board of Revenue) isn’t allowed to work freely without any external pressure. If it is allowed to work freely, it can restore Pakistan’s economy to a somewhat stable position.

Making Ways for Foreign Investment

If a country is heavily invested by foreign countries, it ultimately means that country is on the right track towards a stable economy. Pakistan’s economy faces a crisis of proper foreign investment. The reason behind that is political instability and corruption. The government should make sure that investment of foreign countries is used in the right place and for the betterment of Pakistan’s economy. UNCTAD reported that Pakistan’s foreign direct investment increased from $2.048 billion in 2023 to $2.568 billion in 2024, demonstrating the potential for foreign investment to contribute to domestic economic activity.[5]

Conclusion

So, by improving policies and working on these loop holes, Pakistan’s economy can definitely be improved. It just requires adequate time, proper management and the cooperation of Pakistani people. We should remember that nothing is impossible until we give up. Pakistan’s economy will be a future giant if it is given proper attention by the government and of course by the people. Pakistan is a very fertile land and its people are very much hard working. They just need established policies that would change the course of their economy.
